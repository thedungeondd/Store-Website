## Sprint Retrospective

### What Went Well ✅

1. **Clear Scope:** Focus on essential tasks (hours and contact info) kept materials simple
2. **Visual Approach:** Screenshot-based guide works well for non-technical users
3. **Multiple Formats:** Slide deck, cheat sheet, and plan address different learning styles
4. **Partner Collaboration:** Regular communication ensured we met actual needs
5. **Account Setup:** GitHub and Instagram accounts prepared ahead of training

### Challenges Faced ⚠️

1. **Scheduling:** Training date not yet confirmed with partner
2. **Screenshot Timing:** Can't create actual screenshots until partner has account access
3. **Instagram Scope:** Minimal documentation as partner already familiar with social media
4. **Technical Balance:** Finding right level of detail for non-technical audience

### Lessons Learned 📚

1. **Simplicity is Key:** Less is more when training non-technical users
2. **Visual Learning:** Screenshots and diagrams are essential
3. **Safety Net:** Emphasizing "you can't break it" reduces user anxiety
4. **Support Access:** Clear support channels increase user confidence

### Improvements for Future Projects 💡

1. Confirm training dates earlier in sprint
2. Create video recordings as backup training material
3. Consider monthly check-ins post-training
4. Build in more time for feedback incorporation

## Academic Reference

This sprint's approach to training and documentation follows best practices outlined in:

**Primary Source:**  
Redish, J. (2014). *Letting Go of the Words: Writing Web Content that Works* (2nd ed.). Morgan Kaufmann.

**Key Principles Applied:**
- **User-Centered Design:** Materials designed for specific audience (non-technical users)
- **Plain Language:** Avoiding jargon and technical terminology
- **Visual Communication:** Using screenshots and diagrams to support learning
- **Chunking Information:** Breaking complex tasks into manageable steps
- **Accessibility:** Multiple formats to accommodate different learning styles

**Additional Supporting Concepts:**
- Adult learning theory (andragogy)
- Knowledge transfer best practices
- Technical communication for non-technical audiences
- User experience (UX) documentation standards
